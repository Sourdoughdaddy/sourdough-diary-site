The Library / When it goes wrong

Gummy crumb has three quite different causes

Cut too early, underbaked, or high enzyme activity. They look similar and want opposite fixes.

Why

Cut too early and the starch hasn't finished setting: wait longer, change nothing else. Underbaked and the centre never reached temperature: bake longer or hotter. High enzyme activity, common with sprouted grain or a lot of rye, means starch is being broken into sugars faster than it can set. That one needs more acidity or less of the offending flour, and baking longer makes it worse.

Further down

Rye's answer to this is acidity itself: a properly sour rye ferment inhibits the amylase that would otherwise destroy the crumb. It's the clearest case in baking of sourness being functional rather than a flavour preference.

Try it

If it's gummy, wait two hours and cut again before changing anything. Most of the time that's the whole answer.

  • The loaf that came out flatA loaf that spreads into a disc has usually been fermented too far, shaped too loosely, or made too wet for its flour.
  • Big holes, dense everywhere elseA few huge caverns in an otherwise tight, gummy crumb is under-fermentation, however open it looks at first glance.
  • A crust that stays palePale crust is not enough heat, not enough time uncovered, or a dough that had no sugar left to brown with.
  • A burnt bottomThe base of a pot or stone is much hotter than the air around the loaf. Put something between the two.
