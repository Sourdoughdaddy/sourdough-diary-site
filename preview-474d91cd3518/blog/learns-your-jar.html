What "it learns your jar" means

Every sourdough recipe says "use your starter at its peak". Almost none can say when that is, because it depends on your jar, your flour and your kitchen.

Sourdough Diary makes a prediction and then lets you correct it.

Where the first guess comes from

When you set up a starter, the app asks one thing about its timing: how long it takes to peak after an equal feed, one part starter to one part flour and one part water, in your kitchen. That number is the anchor.

From there, two things move the clock.

  • How much you feed it. More food per spoonful of starter takes longer to get through. Not in proportion, though: four times the food takes about twice as long.
  • How warm the kitchen is. Fermentation roughly doubles in speed for every ten degrees. A starter that peaks in eight hours at 20°C is ready in about four at 30°C.

Where it gets better

A guess about a living thing is still a guess. So when the jar peaks, you can tell the app, with one tap. It compares that with what it predicted and moves the anchor. The next prediction starts from your jar's real behaviour.

After a few corrections, "ready around 16:30" means something.

What it is used for

The same prediction runs the rest of the app. The planner uses it to say when to feed so the starter is ready when you want to mix. The levain feed is sized and timed from it. And the note on a loaf that went wrong can say "the starter went in two hours before its peak", because the app knows when the peak was.
