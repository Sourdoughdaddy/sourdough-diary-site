A sourdough glossary, in plain terms.

The words you meet in recipes and in the app.

ABCDFHILOPS

A

Autolyse
Mixing only flour and water and letting them rest before adding starter and salt. The flour hydrates and gluten begins to form on its own.
Autolyse, fermentolyse, or neither

B

Banneton
The basket a shaped loaf proofs in. It holds the shape and leaves the flour rings on the crust.
Stuck to the basket
Bench rest
A short rest between pre-shape and final shape, so the dough relaxes and can be shaped without tearing.
Bulk fermentation
The first rise of the whole dough, from the moment the starter goes in until it is divided or shaped. Most of the flavour and structure is built here.
How a bake runs

C

Coil fold
A gentler fold: lifting the dough from the middle so the ends tuck underneath. Used for wet doughs.
Cold proof
A final proof in the fridge, usually overnight. It slows fermentation, deepens the flavour and makes the loaf easier to score.
How a bake runs
Crumb
The inside of the loaf. Open means large, irregular holes; tight means small and even.
Big holes, dense everywhere elseGummy crumb

D

Discard
The starter you remove before feeding so the jar does not keep growing. It can go into pancakes, crackers and the like.
Dutch oven
A heavy lidded pot. Baking in it traps the loaf's own steam, which helps oven spring and crust.
Ice cubes for steamA burnt bottom

F

Feeding ratio
Starter to flour to water, by weight. 1:5:5 means one part starter, five parts flour and five parts water. A bigger ratio gives the starter more food and takes longer to peak.
Starter calculatorWhich feeding ratio?
Fermentolyse
Like autolyse, but with the starter already mixed in. Fermentation starts during the rest.
Autolyse, fermentolyse, or neither
Final proof
The last rise of the shaped loaf, in its basket, before baking.
Underproofed or overproofed

H

Hooch
The grey liquid that collects on a hungry starter. It is alcohol from fermentation. Stir it in or pour it off, then feed.
Hydration
Water as a percentage of the flour's weight. 750 g water on 1000 g flour is 75% hydration. Wetter doughs are harder to handle and can give a more open crumb.
High hydration and open crumb

I

Inoculation
How much starter is in a dough, as a percentage of the flour. 20% is common. Less starter means a slower, longer fermentation.

L

Levain
The portion of ripe starter that goes into a dough, usually built for that bake by feeding a little starter the evening before.
Starter calculator

O

Oven spring
The fast rise in the first minutes of baking, as the gas in the dough expands before the crust sets.
The loaf that came out flat

P

Peak
The moment a fed starter has risen as far as it will, domed and full of bubbles, just before it starts to fall. The best time to use it.
Starter calculatorDoes it have to double?
Poke test
Pressing a floured finger into the proofed loaf. If the dent springs back slowly and not all the way, it is ready to bake.
Underproofed or overproofed
Pre-shape
Loosely rounding the dough after bulk so it holds together before the final shape.

S

Scoring
Cutting the top of the loaf just before baking, so it opens where you choose and not where it is weakest.
Starter
A culture of wild yeast and bacteria kept alive in flour and water. It is what makes sourdough rise and gives it its flavour.
The starter in the app
Stretch and fold
Pulling one side of the dough up and folding it over, turning the bowl and repeating. Done a few times during bulk to build strength without kneading.
