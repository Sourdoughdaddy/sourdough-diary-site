The Library / Myths, tested

Autolyse, fermentolyse, or everything at once

Three ways to start a dough, argued about far more than the difference deserves. All three make good bread.

Why

A true autolyse is flour and water only, resting before anything else goes in: the flour hydrates and gluten begins to form without fermentation running. A fermentolyse adds the starter at the start, so the rest is also the beginning of bulk. All at once means starter and salt go in from the first minute. At home, with long fermentation doing most of the gluten work anyway, the results are close.

Further down

Where a real autolyse earns its place: whole-grain doughs, where the bran needs time to soften; very wet doughs, which get easier to handle; and short timelines. The one thing that genuinely changes is the clock. With a fermentolyse, bulk has already started while you wait, so count that time. See the Technique Studio for the two-way comparison.

Try it

Pick the one that fits your day and keep it the same for three loaves. Consistency teaches you more than the method does.

More from this shelf

  • “Dissolve the starter in the water first”You can, and it does no harm. But you don't have to, and whisking it until frothy adds nothing.
  • “Metal kills your starter”A stainless steel spoon, bowl or whisk does nothing to a starter. Stir away.
  • “A hundred-year-old starter makes better bread”The story is lovely. The microbes don't know about it.
  • “You need high hydration for an open crumb”Water helps, but fermentation and handling decide most of it. A 70% dough can be beautifully open; an 85% one can be dense.
  • “Tap water will kill your starter”For an established starter, tap water is almost always fine.
  • “It has to double before you can use it”Doubling is a useful sign for a liquid wheat starter. For others it's the wrong test.
  • “Throw ice cubes in the pot for steam”In a closed pot, the dough makes all the steam it needs. The ice is optional at best.
  • “Sourdough is fine if you can't eat gluten”It is not gluten-free, and it is not safe for coeliac disease. Some people do find it easier to digest, for a different reason.
