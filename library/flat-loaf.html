The Library / When it goes wrong

The loaf that came out flat

A loaf that spreads into a disc has usually been fermented too far, shaped too loosely, or made too wet for its flour.

Why

Each cause leaves a clue. Overproofed dough is slack and sticky when it comes out of the basket, collapses when scored, and bakes pale with a tight, even crumb. A loosely shaped loaf has good, lively dough that simply had no tension to hold it up: the crumb is open but the loaf is low. A dough too wet for its flour never held a shape at any point.

Further down

A weak or unripe starter can give a flat loaf too, but that one is dense and gummy as well, with a few large holes: under-fermented, not over. After a bake, answer the three questions in the app and it reads them against that bake's own log to say which is most likely.

Try it

Change one thing next time, not three. Otherwise the next loaf can't tell you which one mattered.
