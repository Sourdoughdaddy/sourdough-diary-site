The Library / When it goes wrong

Underproofed and overproofed look different in the oven

One bursts open at a random seam. The other spreads flat and barely springs. The bake tells you which mistake you made.

Why

An underproofed loaf still has plenty of gas-producing capacity left, so it expands violently in the oven and tears wherever it's weakest, often ignoring your score entirely. An overproofed loaf has exhausted much of that capacity and its gluten has been softened by accumulated acid, so it slumps sideways instead of upwards and the score stays shallow.

Further down

Underproofing usually gives a tight, uneven crumb with large irregular tunnels near the top. Overproofing gives a crumb that's uniformly small and slightly dense, sometimes with a visible flat area where it settled. Two different failures, two different pictures.

Try it

Photograph the crumb of every loaf, good and bad. Three or four in, you'll be able to diagnose your own bakes at a glance.

  • Big holes, dense everywhere elseA few huge caverns in an otherwise tight, gummy crumb is under-fermentation, however open it looks at first glance.
  • A crust that stays palePale crust is not enough heat, not enough time uncovered, or a dough that had no sugar left to brown with.
  • A burnt bottomThe base of a pot or stone is much hotter than the air around the loaf. Put something between the two.
