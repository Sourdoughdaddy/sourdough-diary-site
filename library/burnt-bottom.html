The Library / When it goes wrong

A burnt bottom

The base of a pot or stone is much hotter than the air around the loaf. Put something between the two.

Why

Cast iron and stone store heat and deliver it straight into the bottom of the loaf, which is what gives the spring. It's also why the base can scorch before the top is done, especially on a low shelf near the bottom element.

Further down

The fixes are all about insulation. Slide an empty baking tray onto the shelf below the pot. Put a sheet of baking paper and a sprinkle of semolina or coarse flour under the loaf. Move the pot up a shelf. Or, for the uncovered half of the bake, lift the loaf out of the pot and finish it directly on the oven rack.

  • Underproofed and overproofed look different in the ovenOne bursts open at a random seam. The other spreads flat and barely springs. The bake tells you which mistake you made.
  • Gummy crumb has three quite different causesCut too early, underbaked, or high enzyme activity. They look similar and want opposite fixes.
  • The loaf that came out flatA loaf that spreads into a disc has usually been fermented too far, shaped too loosely, or made too wet for its flour.
  • Big holes, dense everywhere elseA few huge caverns in an otherwise tight, gummy crumb is under-fermentation, however open it looks at first glance.
  • A crust that stays palePale crust is not enough heat, not enough time uncovered, or a dough that had no sugar left to brown with.
  • Stuck to the basketRice flour, more of it than seems necessary, and a dough that went in with a dry, floured surface.
