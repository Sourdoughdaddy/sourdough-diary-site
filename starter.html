A starter with a name

It learns your jar, then tells you when it is ready.

Knows when it peaks

A prediction for this jar, in this kitchen.

It starts from the feeding ratio and your kitchen's temperature. Tell it when the jar really peaked, and the next prediction is closer.

A jar with opinions

It keeps a diary. It is faintly unimpressed.

Tap the jar and it answers. On Sunday evening it writes you a letter about its week. Keep two, and they have views on each other.

Dress the jar

Cloth, cork or a crown. Twelve colours. Pearls, if it has earned them.

A starter has a family

Most starters were given by someone, or will be given to someone.

Give some away and the jar keeps its place in the tree. Everyone who has a piece can see where the family has spread, and only they can.

Room for the whole shelf

More than one starter

A shelf of jars, each with its own reminders.

What it is fed on

Wheat, rye or spelt: each jar says what it lives on.

The fridge, and holidays

Rest it in the cold, with a plan for when you are away.

And the small things

  • A star signFrom its birthday, with a horoscope in the Sunday letter.
  • A postcard from the fridgeIt writes when it has been in the cold a while.
  • Dressed for the occasionA scarf in the fridge, sunglasses in the heat, a hat on its birthday.
  • Little soundsA bubble, a burp. One switch turns them off.
  • Build a levainSay how much you need and when. It says what to weigh.
  • Retired jarsA jar that is gone stays in the family tree.
