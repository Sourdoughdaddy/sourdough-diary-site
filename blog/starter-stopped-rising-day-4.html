Why did my sourdough starter stop rising on day 4?

Your starter is not dead. A new starter that rose well on day 2 or 3 and then went flat around day 4 is doing what nearly every new starter does. The first rise came from bacteria that do not survive in a sour jar, and the yeast that will raise your bread has not taken over yet. Keep feeding it.

What the first rise was

Flour carries many kinds of microbes. In the first days, the fast ones win. Bacteria such as Leuconostoc make a lot of gas quickly, so the jar doubles and often smells bad while it does.

That rise looks like a finished starter. It is a false start.

Why it goes quiet

Those early bacteria make acid, and they cannot live in the acid they make. As the jar turns sour they die off, and the rise goes with them.

What is left is a handover. The acid-tolerant yeasts and the lactic acid bacteria that make up a real sourdough starter are in there, but they are slow to get going. Until there are enough of them, the jar sits flat. It may smell sharp, like vinegar or even nail polish remover. That is normal at this stage.

What to do

  • Keep feeding once a day. Discard all but 25 g, then add 25 g flour and 25 g lukewarm water.
  • Do not start over. A fresh jar puts you back at day 1 with the same quiet days ahead.
  • Keep it warm, around 24 to 26°C if you can. A cool kitchen can double the wait.
  • Do not feed more often to wake it up. Extra feeds dilute the yeast you are waiting for.
  • Some wholegrain or rye flour in the feed helps. It brings more microbes and more for them to eat.

How long the quiet lasts

Usually two to four days. In a cool kitchen it can be a week or more.

The first sign that it is ending is the smell. It turns from sharp to something closer to beer or yoghurt. Small bubbles come back, and then the jar starts to rise again after each feed, more slowly than the false start but on time, day after day.

A starter is ready to bake with when it doubles after a feed and does so several days in a row. For most people that is somewhere between day 7 and day 14.

When it really has gone wrong

Pink or orange streaks, or fuzzy mould on the surface, mean the jar should be thrown out. A grey or brown liquid on top does not. That is hooch: the starter is hungry. Stir it in or pour it off, and feed.
