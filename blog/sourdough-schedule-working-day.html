A sourdough schedule for a working day

You can bake sourdough on a working day if two things happen while you are not there: the starter rises while you are at work, and the loaf proofs in the fridge while you sleep. Feed at 7:30, mix at 18:15, shape by 23:15, bake the next day.

The times below assume a kitchen at 22°C and a starter that peaks four hours after an equal feed. Yours will differ, and the last section says how to move them.

The timeline

WhenWhatHow long
7:30Feed the starter 1:7:710 h 35 to peak
18:15Mix flour and water, leave it30 min
18:45Add starter and salt. Bulk starts here4 h
19:15 to 20:45Four folds, half an hour apartinside the bulk
22:45Pre-shape, then let it rest30 min
23:15Shape, into the basket, into the fridge10 min
Next dayHeat the oven, bake straight from the fridge30 min + 45 min

Why 1:7:7 in the morning

An equal feed, 1:1:1, peaks in about four hours. Fed at 7:30, that starter is at its best at 11:30 and long past it when you walk in the door.

More food per gram of starter takes longer to eat through. One part starter to seven parts flour and seven parts water takes around ten and a half hours at 22°C, which lands at about six in the evening. For one loaf that is 10 g starter, 70 g flour and 70 g water: 150 g, of which 100 g goes into the dough and the rest back into the jar.

The other ratios and their times are in which feeding ratio to use, and the starter calculator does the sum for your own jar.

The evening

Bulk fermentation is one clock. It starts when the starter goes into the dough and ends at the pre-shape, and the folds happen inside it. At 22°C with 100 g of starter in 500 g of flour it takes about four hours, so a dough that gets its starter at 18:45 is ready to shape around 22:45.

After the fourth fold at 20:45 the dough needs nothing from you for two hours.

Go by the dough before the clock. It should have grown by about half, feel airy, and wobble when you shake the bowl. If it has not, give it another half hour.

The next day

The fridge is what makes this fit. A shaped loaf keeps there for anything between eight hours and a day, and it bakes straight from cold.

  • Before work: oven on at 6:30, bread in at 7:00, out at 7:45. That is under eight hours in the fridge, which is on the short side but works.
  • After work: oven on at 18:00, bread in at 18:30. Nineteen hours in the fridge gives a more sour loaf. Do not go much past a day.

If your kitchen is not 22°C

Temperature moves everything. Ten degrees roughly doubles the speed: a four hour bulk at 22°C is around three hours at 26°C and closer to six at 16°C. The starter moves the same way.

  • Warm kitchen: feed 1:10:10 in the morning so the starter is not past its peak by evening, and expect to shape earlier.
  • Cool kitchen: feed 1:5:5, and put the dough somewhere warm, such as the oven with only the light on, or the evening gets very late.
  • Home later than six: a starter a little past its peak still raises bread. An hour late is not a reason to skip the bake.
