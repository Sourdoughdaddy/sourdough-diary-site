Same-day sourdough, without the fridge

Yes, you can bake sourdough in one day. Skip the night in the fridge and let the shaped loaf proof on the counter for about an hour and a half. Feed the starter the night before, mix at seven, and the bread comes out of the oven at a quarter past two.

The times assume a kitchen at 22°C and a starter that peaks four hours after an equal feed.

The proof on the counter

This is the step that replaces the fridge, and it is the one to watch. At room temperature the loaf keeps fermenting at full speed, so the window is short. Around ninety minutes at 22°C is typical. In a warm kitchen it can be an hour.

Use the poke test. Press a floured finger a centimetre into the dough:

  • It springs back at once: not ready. Give it another twenty minutes.
  • It comes back slowly and leaves a small dent: bake now.
  • The dent stays: it has gone a little too far. Bake it straight away.

Underproofed or overproofed shows what each one looks like after baking.

What you give up

  • Sourness. The long cold night is where much of the tang comes from. A same-day loaf is milder.
  • Easy scoring. Cold dough is firm and the blade glides. Warm dough is soft and drags. Use a fresh blade and one confident cut.
  • Slack in the timing. A loaf in the fridge waits for you for hours. A loaf on the counter does not.

What you keep is a properly fermented loaf with a good crumb. The fridge is a convenience and a flavour choice. The bread does not need it.

Making it faster still

  • More starter. 150 g for 500 g of flour brings the bulk from four hours to about three. See how much starter goes in the dough.
  • A warm spot. At 26°C a four hour bulk takes about three. The oven with only the light on is usually around there.

Do both and bread by lunch is realistic. The dough moves fast at that pace, so check it early.
